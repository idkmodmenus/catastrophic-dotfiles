What's in the shell

Everything lives in the bar, and it all opens on hover

Built with Quickshell on Hyprland. Menus drop in as glass cards with a light motion blur while they move.

Glass island bar

Three floating islands, tinted like the kitty terminal and blurred by Hyprland. Nothing runs down the sides of the screen.

Cat workspaces

A dot for an empty workspace, a paw where windows are open and a cat on the one you are on. Scroll the island to switch.

Apps in the bar

Pinned and running apps next to the clock. Click to focus, again to cycle windows, middle-click for a new one. The app grid sits at the end.

Super + D

Dashboard

Your version, update status and what's new, with the calendar, media and system load. Rest on the clock to open it.

Hover

Popouts for every chip

Wi-Fi networks, Bluetooth devices, a mixer for each app, battery and the player all drop in under the item you point at.

matugen

Colours from your wallpaper

Pick a wallpaper and the shell, dock, terminal, launcher, GTK and Qt apps recolour to match, in light or dark.

Super + `

Drop-down terminal

Terminals share one kitty process, so each extra window costs about 11 MB instead of a whole new kitty.

Privacy

Mic, camera and screen dots

While an app uses the microphone, camera or screen sharing, a coloured dot shows in the bar. Hover it to see which app.

Super + N

Its own notifications

The shell serves notifications itself, with action buttons such as the update prompt and a Silent mode on the bell.

Runs on

Made for Arch, tuned on CachyOS

The installer checks for one of these before it starts, and works on a plain Arch install without a desktop.

Install

One command on a fresh or existing Arch system

Run it as your normal user. It asks for your password once and before every system-level change.

kitty
$ curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/idkmodmenus/catastrophic-dotfiles/main/install.sh | bash
  1. Checks your system. Arch or CachyOS and an internet connection.
  2. Installs the desktop. Offers a full system update first, then Hyprland and everything it needs.
  3. Adds the apps. Window overview, settings app, walker and elephant, which start the first time you use them.
  4. Links your dotfiles. Copies them to ~/.mydotfiles and backs up anything it replaces.
  5. Asks before system changes. Services, SDDM, the NVIDIA driver, icons and the boot splash.
